Design philosophy

PRINCIPLES

01
Hierarchy & Contrast

Case Study — Mobile App Design

VIBELY

2025
Figma Mobile Events UX Research

The problem

FRAGMENTED
EVENT DISCOVERY

80%
of event discovery happens via scattered WhatsApp forwards
0
dedicated event platforms designed for the Jamaican market
47%
of users miss events they would have attended if they'd known

The brief

Jamaicans, especially in Montego Bay and Kingston, rely on scattered WhatsApp forwards and Instagram story reposts to find out about events. Promoters have no reliable channel and attendees miss out constantly.

The goal was to design a centralized, trust-first event discovery app tailored to the Jamaican social calendar — nightlife, concerts, food events, and community gatherings — with a visual identity bold enough to stand out on a phone screen.

Key constraint: the user base is mobile-only, largely 18–35, and has zero tolerance for friction in the booking or discovery flow.

How I got there

DESIGN PROCESS

01
DISCOVER
Surveyed 20+ local event-goers. Mapped pain points around discovery, ticket scams, and no-show events. Identified trust as the #1 barrier.
02
DEFINE
Personas for 3 user types: casual attendee, serious nightlifer, promoter. Built user journey maps highlighting drop-off points in existing flows.
03
DESIGN
Low-fi wireframes → mid-fi feedback loops → high-fidelity in Figma. Dark theme selected immediately — nightlife context demands it.
04
TEST
5 usability sessions. Key insight: users skipped the categories — they wanted the feed first. Redesigned home screen around this behaviour.

Design rationale

KEY DECISIONS

Visual Language
Dark Theme, Warm Accents
Events happen at night. The dark UI isn't aesthetic choice alone — it reduces eye strain in low-light environments and makes event cover photos pop without competing backgrounds. The warm orange accent signals energy and social warmth without being aggressive.
Information Architecture
Feed First, Browse Second
Testing revealed users wanted serendipitous discovery, not intentional search. The home screen leads with a swipeable card feed of upcoming events near you — categories become a secondary browsing mode, not the entry point.
Trust Design
Verified Promoter Badges
Ticket scams are real in Jamaica. Adding verified status, sold ticket counts, and a review layer directly on the event card rebuilt confidence before users even tapped through. This one feature reduced drop-off at the ticket screen significantly in testing.
Social Layer
"Who's Going" Visibility
Social proof is the strongest conversion signal in entertainment. Showing mutual friends attending an event — or the total attendee count — taps directly into FOMO. This feature drove the highest increase in intent-to-attend in usability testing.

Case Study — Mobile App Design

FIXUPJA

2025
Figma Mobile Services UX Flow

The problem

FINDING HELP IS
WORD OF MOUTH

Jamaica has chronic, unaddressed infrastructure problems such as potholes, broken streetlights, blocked drains with no centralized system to report them.
Citizens default to complaining on social media instead of reaching authorities, meaning complaints generate noise but no action.
Government agencies (NWA, KSAC, parish councils) have zero real-time visibility into what needs fixing and where, so they're essentially flying blind.

The brief

FixUpJa is a mobile-friendly civic reporting app that bridges the gap between Jamaican citizens and government maintenance agencies.

The core loop: a citizen spots a problem (pothole, broken light, flooded drain), submits a photo + GPS pin through the app, and the report is automatically routed to the correct government body, NWA, KSAC, or the relevant parish council via a formatted email system. Government admins then assign work to crews, who track and close jobs from a mobile view. Citizens get status updates throughout.

Three completely different user types had to be designed for within a single product: Citizens (report & track), Government Officials (dashboard, assignment, PR), and Maintenance Crews (mobile work orders, completion photos). Community groups are a fourth type that they can view area issues, vote on priorities, and generate reports for grant applications.

How I got there

DESIGN PROCESS

01
DISCOVER
Mapped three entirely separate user journeys (citizen, admin, crew) and their conflicting needs. The core tension: citizens want speed and anonymity; government wants accountability and verification; crews want simplicity.
02
DEFINE
Established the legal and privacy framework first for photo guidelines, blur tools, GPS verification, Terms of Service, and consent checkboxes. Civic apps live or die on trust and legal clarity. These weren't afterthoughts; they shaped the entire submission flow.
03
DESIGN
Report submission flow, interactive colour-coded map (red = new, yellow = in progress, green = fixed), admin dashboard, and crew mobile view were designed as four distinct interface modes within one product.
04
SAFEGUARD
Built anti-abuse into the Information Architecture itself, GPS verification required, 20+ character minimum descriptions, admin review queue, duplicate detection, and repeat offender blocking. The system had to be resistant to spam from the ground up.

Design rationale

KEY DECISIONS

Privacy & Legal Design
Photo Privacy Tutorial on First Use
Taking photos in public spaces in Jamaica carries real legal and safety risk. Rather than burying guidelines in a terms page, a mandatory tutorial appears on first launch walking users through what to avoid (faces, plates, house numbers) and offering a blur tool for accidental exposures. This makes compliance feel helpful, not punitive.
Core Interface
Colour-Coded Map as the Core UI
The interactive map (red/yellow/green pins) is the product's most powerful transparency tool. Citizens can see their report isn't just sitting in a void, it has a status. Government can see geographic clustering of issues. Media and community groups can screenshot it for advocacy. One visual layer serves four user types simultaneously.
System Architecture
Email Routing Instead of API Integration
Connecting directly into government IT systems (NWA, KSAC) would require bureaucratic approvals and infrastructure access that isn't realistic at this stage. Instead, the app auto-generates a formatted report email sent to the correct agency address. Citizens get a "Report Sent" confirmation. It's low-tech by design and it actually works within Jamaica's institutional reality.
Retention Strategy
Gamification for Sustained Engagement
Civic apps die when initial enthusiasm fades. Adding leaderboards (top reporters, best crews), achievement badges, and an upvote system for high-priority issues creates intrinsic motivation to keep reporting and gives maintenance crews public recognition for their work, which is a meaningful incentive in a public-sector context.

Case Study — Website UI Design

DECATUR
TREE
SERVICES

2025
Figma Web UI Local Business Conversion

The problem

NO DIGITAL
PRESENCE,
NO LEADS

76%
of customers research a local service business online before calling
8s
average time a visitor takes to decide whether to stay or leave a site
3×
more leads generated by local businesses with professional web presence

The brief

Decatur Tree Services is a local tree removal and trimming company relying entirely on referrals and yard signs for new business. Competitors in the Decatur area have professional websites while Decatur Tree Services had nothing — making them invisible to the growing share of customers who search before they call.

The design challenge was to create a high-converting, trust-forward website that communicates professionalism and safety compliance instantly — within the first scroll — while making it dead simple for a homeowner to request a quote.

Secondary goal: the site needed to work flawlessly on mobile, where most local service searches happen, and load fast even on average connections.

How I got there

DESIGN PROCESS

01
COMPETE
Audited top 5 local competitors. Identified the patterns that work (before/after photos, license badges, fast quote CTAs) and the gaps (mobile UX, page speed, clear pricing hierarchy).
02
STRUCTURE
Information hierarchy designed around one goal: get the visitor to the quote form. Every content block is sequenced to answer objections: "Are they professional? Are they licensed? Can I see their work?"
03
DESIGN
Deep green brand palette — natural, trustworthy, outdoors. Bold typography to cut through the visual noise of crowded SERPs. High-contrast CTAs above the fold on every device.
04
MOBILE
Designed mobile-first. The "Get Free Quote" button appears sticky on scroll for mobile users. Phone number is tap-to-call everywhere. No wasted horizontal space on small screens.

Design rationale

KEY DECISIONS

Above the Fold
Trust Signals Before Services
Tree removal is a high-anxiety purchase — homeowners worry about damage and scams. The hero section leads with license numbers, insurance badges, and years in business — before listing a single service. You buy the company before you buy the service.
Conversion Design
One CTA, Everywhere
Multiple competing CTAs ("Call us", "Get a Quote", "Book Now") create decision paralysis. Every clickable element on the site leads to a single outcome: the quote request form. Sticky on mobile scroll, pinned in desktop nav. One path, no exceptions.
Social Proof
Before/After Gallery Placement
Before/after photos are the single most persuasive content for local service businesses — they prove capability without claims. Placing the gallery immediately after the hero (before pricing or services) addresses the "can they actually do the job?" objection first.
Service Pages
Each Service Gets Its Own Story
Homepage gives the overview; dedicated service pages ("Tree Removal", "Stump Grinding", "Emergency Services") capture specific search intent and allow for targeted local SEO. Each page repeats the trust badges and quote CTA so no page is a dead end.

Case Study — Web Design & Brand

BLAZE
BARBER
SHOP

2026
Framer Web Design Branding Booking UX

The problem

INVISIBLE
IN A
DIGITAL AGE

100%
of bookings were walk-in only — zero digital presence to capture intent
0
premium barbershop websites serving the Montego Bay market
3×
more bookings generated by service businesses with professional web presence

The brief

Blaze Barbershop launched in 2024 in Montego Bay with a clear identity — premium grooming, precision craft, no compromise. But without a website, they were invisible to anyone who wasn't already a regular. New clients searching for a quality cut in MoBay found nothing.

The goal was to build a high-end web presence that matched the quality of the in-shop experience — showcasing the team, services, and pricing while making it effortless for a client to book a session. Built entirely in Framer for fast deployment and easy client edits.

Key constraint: the design had to feel premium without feeling inaccessible. Blaze serves everyday Jamaicans who take their appearance seriously — not a luxury spa clientele. The tone needed to be sharp, confident, and welcoming.

How I got there

DESIGN PROCESS

01
BRAND AUDIT
Reviewed existing barbershop websites globally and locally. Found that Jamaican barbershops had zero digital design quality — a wide open gap to own the premium positioning.
02
CONTENT MAP
Defined the five core sections: hero statement, services with pricing, team profiles, testimonials, and a persistent booking CTA. Every section answers a specific client objection before they leave.
03
VISUAL DESIGN
Dark background, gold typography accents, high-contrast editorial photography. The aesthetic borrows from luxury fashion and premium grooming brands — deliberate elevation above the typical barbershop site.
04
BUILD
Built in Framer for zero-code deployment and client autonomy. Smooth scroll animations, responsive layout, and a sticky booking CTA that persists on every viewport without feeling intrusive.

Design rationale

KEY DECISIONS

Visual Identity
Dark Luxury Over Bright & Generic
Most barbershop websites default to white backgrounds, clip-art scissors, and generic stock photos. Going dark with gold accents immediately signals premium positioning and stands out in every search result. The aesthetic does the selling before a word is read.
Conversion Design
Persistent "Book Your Session" CTA
A barbershop website has one job: get the client to book. The CTA appears in the hero, at the bottom of the page, and as a sticky element in the navigation. It follows the user through every section so the decision to book is never more than one tap away.
Trust Architecture
Team Profiles Over Generic "Our Staff"
Clients choose barbers, not barbershops. Giving Marcus, Jordan, and Andre each a named profile with their specialty (Master Barber, Style Specialist, Fade Artist) lets clients form a preference before they even walk in — driving return visits and specific requests.
Pricing Transparency
Visible "From $X" on Every Service
Barbershops that hide pricing create anxiety, especially for first-time clients. Displaying starting prices directly on each service card removes that barrier — clients arrive knowing what to expect, which means fewer awkward conversations and higher walk-in conversion.

Case Study — Web Design & Brand

VOYAGE
VACATIONS

2026
Framer Web Design Luxury Travel

The problem

LOOKING
BUDGET IN A
LUXURY MARKET

78%
of luxury travelers research agencies online before making first contact
0.05s
is all it takes for a visitor to form a first impression of your brand
3×
higher lifetime value of premium travel clients vs. standard bookers

The brief

Voyagé operates in the premium end of the travel market — curated escapes, overwater villas, private alpine retreats, and Santorini cruises. The clientele they serve expects white-glove service from the first touchpoint. Their previous digital presence didn't reflect that — it looked like every other travel site, indistinguishable from budget booking platforms.

The goal was to design a website that functioned as a luxury destination in itself — before the trip begins, the site should feel like the first-class lounge. Unhurried, refined, beautiful. Every design decision had to signal premium positioning without saying the word "luxury" once.

Built entirely in Framer to allow fast deployment, smooth editorial scroll animations, and easy client content updates — without sacrificing the visual quality the brand demands.

How I got there

DESIGN PROCESS

01
AUDIT
Studied top luxury travel brands — Virtuoso, Abercrombie & Kent, Jacada Travel. Found they were content-heavy and complicated. The gap: a clean, editorial approach that lets the destinations do the selling.
02
STRIP DOWN
Removed everything that wasn't essential. No price tables on the homepage. No bullet-point service lists. Just three things: destinations, proof, and a single CTA. Restraint is the luxury signal.
03
DESIGN
Generous white space, full-bleed travel photography, minimal serif typography. The design deliberately recedes — its job is to frame the imagery, not compete with it. Every layout decision prioritises the photography.
04
BUILD
Built in Framer with smooth scroll-triggered reveals and mobile-first responsive breakpoints. The animations are slow and cinematic — reinforcing the unhurried luxury tone throughout the browsing experience.

Design rationale

KEY DECISIONS

Visual Language
White Space as a Luxury Signal
Luxury brands don't cram information. Space is a deliberate design choice — it signals confidence, restraint, and premium positioning before the visitor reads a single word. The page breathes. That breathing is the brand.
Content Strategy
Photography First, Copy Second
The destinations are the product. Every section is engineered to showcase travel photography at maximum scale, with minimal copy that supports rather than competes with the visuals. The Maldives image doesn't need a paragraph — it needs room.
Conversion Design
No Pricing on the Hero
Displaying prices on the homepage of a luxury travel brand is the equivalent of a Michelin-star restaurant putting the menu in the window. The "Book Now" CTA invites a conversation, not a transaction — positioning Voyagé as advisors rather than a booking engine.
Trust Architecture
Specific Testimonials Over Star Ratings
Luxury travel is high-consideration and high-anxiety. Four named clients with specific, detailed stories ("they sourced a private catamaran with a chef") do far more work than five stars. Each testimonial answers a different hesitation a new client would have.
